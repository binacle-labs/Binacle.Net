# Parallel bins

When one algorithm packs several bins, is packing them in parallel faster than one after the other, from what
size, and on how many cores? Version 2, packing.

Every bin is the same, 160x120x80, and every item is the same, 20x15x10, from 1 to 256 pieces per bin. Every
item fits. That is the best case for parallel.

## 📊 Where parallel starts to win

The smallest piece count per bin where parallel is faster even after its wobble: ratio plus RatioSD below 1.
One bin has nothing to run in parallel, so it cannot win.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FFD | 64 | 0.62 to 0.67 | 0.52 to 0.56 | 0.65 to 0.69 | 0.69 to 0.75 |
| FFD | 256 | 0.57 to 0.66 | 0.57 to 0.59 | 0.61 to 0.70 | 0.60 to 0.74 |

| BFD | 256 | 0.61 to 0.63 | 0.58 to 0.64 | 0.56 to 0.61 | 0.38 to 0.59 |

Memory differs by at most 0.02 KB between line counts.
The widest range is WFD, 64 pieces, 2 cores. There the one-after-the-other time is
67, 90, 68, 67 µs at 1, 4, 16, 64 lines.

## ⚠️ What will bite you

- **† marks a ratio below 1 ÷ the smaller of bins and cores.** Two bins or two cores cannot make it more than
  twice as fast, so those cells are noise. That includes every one-bin cell below 1.
- **These runs used a short job** (3 iterations, 1 launch). It blurs ratios near 1.

## 📂 Where the numbers come from

The `Identical_*_Pieces_Packing` and `Identical_*_Lines_Packing` reports in
[benchmarks/baseline/parallel-bins/](benchmarks/baseline/parallel-bins), BenchmarkDotNet on an AMD Ryzen 9
9900X, .NET 10.0.12, each case pinned to its core count.
